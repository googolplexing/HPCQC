Michael Mucciardi · HPCQC × ANIMLL · VTT Q50 (53 qubits) · TFIM 4-Qubit Star · Real Q50 Timing Data (April 7, 2026)

1 Q50 Recap

VTT Q50: 53-qubit superconducting QPU, 82 CZ edges, rotated square lattice. For 4-qubit star (K₁,₃) topology, the placement solver finds 108 unique placements across 36 hub qubits. DSatur graph coloring packs them into 16 non-overlapping rounds (provably optimal). Each round is one composite circuit.

For TFIM, each placement requires 2 measurement circuits (Z-basis + X-basis). So 1 seed = 108 placements × 2 Pauli groups = 216 measurement tasks packed into 32 composite circuits (16 rounds × 2 bases).

Key numbers: 53 qubits · 82 CZ edges · 108 star placements · 16 DSatur rounds · 2 Pauli groups (TFIM) · 32 circuits per seed · 4096 shots per circuit

Table 1 — QPU Phase Timing. Batch: job label (count × circuit type). Circuits: number of circuits submitted in one .run() call. Width: number of physical qubits per circuit. Critical finding: Queue wait dominates (0.02s–42s) and is identical whether you submit 1 circuit or 100. Fixed per-batch overhead (QX + postprocess + delivery) averages ~4s. Per-circuit QPU time is ~1.77s in batches. Every unused circuit slot in a batch is wasted overhead you already paid for.
Width is free: Batch A (4-qubit circuits) and Batch E (53-qubit circuits) show nearly identical per-circuit times (1.786s vs 1.759s) and per-shot times (436µs vs 430µs). DSatur packing has zero QPU time penalty — you pay the same whether 4 qubits or 53 are active.

3 The Throughput Ladder — Corrected with Real Timing

Each level adds one optimization to the previous. All wall times use 20s typical queue wait. The "stars/circuit" column shows how many non-overlapping 4-qubit star subcircuits are packed onto one 53-qubit composite circuit — more stars = more science per QPU shot.

Table 2 — Throughput Ladder. Level: packing strategy name. What changes: the single optimization added at this level vs the previous one. Stars/Circ: number of non-overlapping K₁,₃ subcircuits packed into each composite circuit — this is the "density" of science on the QPU per shot. Circuits: number of composite circuits submitted in one .run() batch call. Placements: unique star placements evaluated in one batch — each yields one energy expectation value; this is the science output. Qubit Util: fraction of the 53 physical qubits that carry science payload (averaged across all circuits). Wall Time: total elapsed seconds from batch submission to result delivery = QX overhead (1.14s) + queue wait (20s) + QPU execute (0.65s + 1.77s × circuits) + postprocess (1.53s) + delivery (1.29s). Throughput: placements evaluated per hour = placements ÷ wall time × 3600. Speedup: throughput multiplier vs Level 0 — how many times more placements per hour this level achieves compared to submitting one star at a time.

5 Cross-Seed Intermixing — Filling the Gaps

Instead of each seed getting its own 16 rounds, placements from multiple seeds fill the idle qubits in sparse rounds. The constraint: no two placements share physical qubits. But Seed 0's Placement #7 and Seed 3's Placement #42 use different qubits — they coexist on the same QPU shot.
